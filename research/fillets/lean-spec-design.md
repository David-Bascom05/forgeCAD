`general-method.md` (the one definition: the ball closing, from the crease graph of the offset body)
and ROADMAP step 5. It does not define fillets afresh. It states how that definition is written in
Lean, on top of the forge-exact proof stack, and what the kernel must change to match it.

## 2. Definitions (`Fillet/Spec.lean`)

These are the §1 objects of general-method.md, written over `Pred (V3 K)`. `d2 x y = |x − y|²`.

| Lean | meaning |
|---|---|
| `oball c r`, `cball c r` | `d2 x c < r²`, `≤ r²` |
| `Free S r c` | `∀ y, S y → r² ≤ d2 y c` (the centre set M) |
| `closing S r` | `fun x => ∀ c, Free S r c → r² ≤ d2 x c`, i.e. ℝ³ minus the free open balls |
| `opening S r` | `compl (closing (compl S) r)` |
| `Gen` | a generator: an open face patch (carrier `Quadric`, `Torus` or `Pipe` with trim `QForm`), an open edge, or a vertex. Faces on one carrier across an edge are one generator (seams and flush splits vanish by definition) |
| `Sheet g r` | the centres at distance r from g on its free side: `c = p + r·n(p)`, `p ∈ g`. A polynomial system: n is not normalised, and `r²·|∇q(p)|² = d2 c p` with a sign |
| `Contact S r c` | `cball c r ∩ S` for `c` on ∂M |
| `Crease S r` | the centres whose contacts are not in one generator's closure |
| `Cap c` | the spherical hull of `Contact c` on the sphere ∂B(c, r) (an arc for two contacts) |
| `Shadow S c` | the points on rays from c through `Cap c`, past r, up to their first hit on S |
| `Reach S E` | Γ_E: the arcs of `Crease` joined to the seeds of the picks, crossing no unpicked concave edge's arc and no end vertex |
| `VType v` | the cyclic word of v's edges over {picked, unpicked} × {concave, convex, smooth} |
| `endRule` | the §1.3 table, as an inductive: `sphere` (all picked, one radius), `mitreOrRunOut` (some unpicked), `outside` (refused with the type) |
| `Fillet S E` | for concave picks, `reg (S ∪ (⋃_{c ∈ Reach} Shadow c) ∪ ends)`; for convex picks the dual. Several radius classes are combined by union of fills, then the convex picks are applied after the concave ones (the fixed order that replaces `minority_first`) |
| `Snap τ` | §1.4: a predicate within τ of zero *is* zero. The spec is stated on τ-snapped input |

**Conic sections.** A conic is not a ball closing. The spec reuses everything except the section:

- `Section := ball | conic (w : K)`, with `0 < w < 1`, where w = ρ/(1 − ρ).
- For one crease arc, the spine c(s), the springs p₁(s) and p₂(s) (the *ball's* contacts, from the
  same crease graph) and the arc's range are those of the ball. The section at s is the rational
  quadratic arc with ends p₁(s) and p₂(s), apex a(s) (where the two faces' tangent planes meet in the
  plane of p₁, c, p₂) and middle weight w. Its fill is the region between that arc and the faces.
- Theorem `conic_circle`: `conic w` equals `ball` exactly when w² = (1 + u₁·u₂)/2, where uᵢ =
  (pᵢ − c)/r. That is w = cos(α/2) for the arc angle α, written without trig. When the faces are not
  orthogonal the circle's w varies along the edge; a constant ρ is a non-circular conic.
- Vertex table for conics: run-out and mitre are the same rows (the mitre is the ⋂ of the two conic
  fills, carried on). The all-picked row has **no** conic corner: refuse with the type, or use the
  named G1 corner spec (§5, item 5). G1 tangency at the springs holds for every w (the arc is tangent
  to the control legs).
- This is the statement the conic-fillet session should implement: same `Reach`, same springs, same
  vertex table, `Section.conic w` in place of the ball arc.

## 3. Theorems

Each has a difficulty: R routine, M medium. Numbers O1–O10 are general-method.md §3.

1. `closing_extensive`, `closing_mono`, `closing_idem`, `opening_dual`. (R)
2. **O2, locality**: `Fillet S E ∩ ball x ρ` depends only on `S ∩ ball x (ρ + 2r)`. (R)
3. **Exact surfaces, one theorem per analytic pair** (the §9 refinement "plain pipe"). Each says:
   the crease is this curve, the springs are its feet, the cap is the arc, and `Shadow` over the arc
   is the region bounded by this surface.
   - `plane_plane`: two planes meeting at dihedral angle in (0, π): the crease is the line where the
     offset planes meet, the blend is a cylinder of radius r about it, and the springs are lines. (R)
   - `plane_cyl_parallel`: a plane parallel to the cylinder's axis gives a line crease and a cylinder.
     `cyl_cyl_parallel`, for parallel axes, gives the same. (R)
   - `plane_cyl_perp`: a plane square to the axis gives a circle crease of radius ρ ± r and a torus
     of major ρ ± r, minor r. When ρ = r on the concave side (P7 = 0) the crease is one point and the
     blend a **sphere**: the kernel's `Pivot`. (R)
   - `coaxial_revolution`: plane⊥axis, cylinder, cone and sphere, pairwise coaxial: the crease is a
     circle (meridian line/circle hits) and the blend a torus. This covers plane–cone and plane–sphere,
     and cylinder–cone and cone–cone coaxial. (R/M)
   - **Correction to the brief**: coaxial cylinder–cylinder pairs give *no* blend. The offsets are
     concentric and never meet. The cylinder–cylinder analytic case is parallel axes (a cylinder), not
     coaxial (a torus).
   - `torus_major_le_r`: a circle crease of radius R ≤ r gives a spindle torus. The closing keeps only
     its outer part, the envelope's self-intersection is cut away, and the blend is still that torus
     trimmed (exact, algebraic of degree 4). (M)
   - `no_analytic`: for a plane oblique to a cylinder or cone, or for crossing cylinders, the crease is
     not a line or circle. A pipe of radius r determines its spine (the spine is its r-focal set), so
     the blend is no cylinder, torus or sphere: a procedural `Pipe`. (M)
4. **G1 at the springs** (`g1_spring`): at each spring the blend's normal equals the face's normal.
   Both equal (p − c)/r. This holds for every crease kind, and for `conic w` too. (R)
5. **The result is the regularised boolean** (`fillet_eq_regBool`): `Fillet S E = regBool union S K`
   (concave) or `regBool diff S K'` (convex), where K is a finite union of arrangement cells bounded by
   the faces, the blend surfaces and the caps. With `CurvedC` the decide-once core then builds exactly
   that. The blend face is exactly the envelope: `blend_face_eq_cap_union`. (M; O1 specialised to the
   generators in the table)
6. **Existence (P7)**: `sheet_regular_iff`. The offset sheet of a face is a smooth manifold on the
   arc iff r is less than the face's focal radius there on the free side (cylinder: r < ρ concave;
   torus: r < minor, or on the outer side; sphere likewise). At equality it is the Pivot degeneracy.
   Past it the sheet has a cuspidal edge, and the kernel **refuses** `Focal { face, at }` (O10 is not
   attempted). The spine's own offset is the `torus_major_le_r` case, which is covered and not refused.
7. **Overflow, decidable (P2/P3)**:
   - `Clear arc g := ∀ s ∈ [s₀, s₁], r² ≤ d2 (C s) g` for each generator g not active on the arc.
   - `overflow_decidable`: for line and circle creases (circles parametrised rationally by t =
     tan(θ/2)) and quadric generators, `¬Clear` is the existence of a root, or a negative value, of a
     univariate polynomial in s with algebraic coefficients. It is decided by `Alg`'s `onePosRoot`
     certificates, and the decision is proven (`clear_spec`).
   - In the spec, overflow is **not** a refusal. The first event on the arc is a crease-graph vertex
     (cliff onset, roll-over, swallowed face), and what follows is defined.
   - The kernel may refuse only with `Unbuilt { event, at }` (a defined case it does not build yet),
     `Undecided { predicate, at }` (a sign straddling ±τ), `Focal` or `VertexType`. Each is an exact
     condition here, so a refusal is specified exactly.
8. **Vertex table** (O3, O5, O6, for planar generators first):
   - `vertex_sphere`: every edge at v picked, one radius, and P4 exposed. Then R ∩ U_v = S ∪ (C_r(W_v)
     \ S), and its new boundary is the spherical n-gon of the n contacts.
   - `vertex_mitre`: ⋂ C_r(W_e) ∩ Cap_v. With cylinders, the cut is the P9 branch through the node
     (two conics).
   - `vertex_runout`: one pick, clipped by S.
   - `glue`: these agree with the body formula on the overlaps.
9. **The executable model is the spec** (`model_correct`): the Lean functions of §4 return the crease,
   springs, surface and clearance decision these theorems define, over ℚ and `Alg.Num`.
10. **Float bridge for the differential test** (`kernel_close`, O9 pattern of loft-ssi): for each
    analytic pair, the f64 closed form the kernel computes differs from the exact one by at most
    k·u·(scale), with k proven per formula. The γ_k bounds are hypotheses, discharged as
    `derive/proofs/Proofs/Rounding.lean` does.

## 4. Executable model and differential test

- `Fillet/Model.lean`: per analytic pair, from the exact rationals of the kernel's f64 frames, it
  gives the crease (line or circle, exactly: algebraic where an offset of a non-unit normal needs
  `|n|`), the springs, the surface's parameters, and `Clear` against a list of generators. It returns
  a typed answer: `analytic`, `pipe`, `focal`, `pivot` or `undecided`.
- Driver `forge-fillet-ref`, in the style of `forge-exact`'s Main.
- `verify/fillet-spec` (outside `kernel/`): a Rust harness dumps, for every fillet edge of the census
  parts and of a random generator (planes, cylinders, cones at random frames and radii), the kernel's
  `Blend` (spine, surface kind and parameters, springs at sample t, and its refusal), as JSON lines.
  The Lean driver re-derives each and reports a disagreement beyond theorem 10's bound, or a kind
  mismatch (for example the kernel's `Pipe` where the spec has a torus).
- Re-judging the census's "wrong" fillets by the spec, not OCCT, uses the spec's fill:
  - prismatic and turned parts: exact cells (Tier B, degree ≤ 4);
  - otherwise: the membership oracle of §5.4 Tier A, certified branch and bound on rationals.
- Where OCCT disagrees with the spec, it is listed per part, with the region.

## 5. What the Rust does today: the rules, and where there is more than one way

Read in `kernel/forge-ops/src/fillet.rs`, `fillet_cliff.rs`, `corner.rs`,
`kernel/forge-geom/src/blend.rs` and `kernel/forge-model/src/lib.rs`.

1. **The surface rule** (`Blend::new`): a line spine gives a Cylinder, and a circle spine with major
   > r + LINEAR gives a Torus. Everything else is a procedural `Pipe`, which also covers circle spines
   with R ≤ r + LINEAR: an exact spindle torus is built as a Pipe. The spine comes from `closed_form`
   (plane against anything through `plane_surface`; parallel cylinders; coaxial surfaces of revolution
   by meridian hits), otherwise from `intcurve::trace`. `blend::closed_form` does not use `meet.rs`
   (torus pairs are traced).
2. **Two ways for one spring**: `Blend::spring` builds the closed form and keeps it only if 4 sample
   points agree within LINEAR, else it builds a procedural `Spring`. That is a fallback by sampling.
3. **The retry chain**: the result depends on which try succeeded first.
   - `fillet` tries {default, seams, straight}, accepting a body whose only problems are
     `DuplicateName`.
   - `fillet_with` retries straight. It retries cliff on `Overflow`/`Corner`/`Mitre`, and again on a
     success whose Euler counts are unsound.
   - `fillet_tried` retries across, then seams, then walk × {(seams, rims)}, then probes whole chains
     for `FreeEnd`.
   - `fillet_picked` runs `minority_first` (recursion), loops `SplitFirst` up to 4 times, turns
     notched into a cliff retry, then tries `reseamed` and `joined_pieces`.
   - Then `forge-model/src/lib.rs::overlapping_rounds` makes a boolean of each group.
4. **Ends** (`Builder::resolve`):
   - one hot edge: `switched_out`, or `run_out` followed by `roll_over` on `Overflow`;
   - two hot edges: `joint(a,b)`, then `joint(b,a)`, then `walk_joint(a,b)`, then `walk_joint(b,a)`.
     The walk is gated by a **work budget** (`walk_tests` = 4000 + 200 per edge; `walk_spend`), so
     whether a part builds depends on a budget, which is not definitive;
   - `mitre`, falling back to `joint`;
   - otherwise `rest_ring` and `corner`;
   - and `pivot_end` for Pivots.
5. **Corners: three rules**. `corner()` first tries `corner::setbacks` (an n-sided G1 patch, set back
   "one radius past where the springs meet the neighbours'", or exactly the sphere when one ball
   touches every face). If that has no answer it uses `ball_setbacks` (a sphere from three offsets).
   User setbacks override either. **The spec picks**: equal radii, all picked and exposed give the
   sphere (theorem 8). Unequal radii or mixed kinds at an all-picked vertex are not a closing. They are
   **one** named G1 corner spec, the patch at setback = one radius past the springs' meeting. It stays,
   so as to break nothing that builds now, but as a single rule with its own statement, not tried
   after the sphere. User setbacks are a parameter of that same rule.
6. **Per-case constructions that are crease-graph vertices**: `cliff`, `switch_support`, `merged`/
   `collapse`, `Pivot`, `across`/`Crossed`, `excursions`/`blocked_beyond`, `mitre`,
   `overlapping_rounds`. Each maps to a vertex type or event as in general-method §5.3, and becomes a
   §9 fast path with a proven guard, or goes.

## 6. Order of work, and what reaches the kernel in this session

As in general-method §6:

1. `Fillet/Spec.lean` (§2), theorems 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7, `Axioms.lean`, and the CI job. The CI job
   is an extension of `forge-exact.yml` (forge-exact has its own Lean job), with `#print axioms`
   checked.
2. `Fillet/Model.lean` and the differential test (theorems 9 and 10). The Rust is fixed where the
   closed forms disagree. Expected first candidates: the spindle-torus-as-Pipe rule, and the
   sampled-spring fallback.
3. Tier A/B reference, and the census re-judged by the spec. OCCT's disagreements are reported.
4. The certified crease-graph builder (Phase 1), with the trace session (`claude/repo-review-mbdrlj-
   trace`), which certifies quadric and torus intersections.
5. Retire the retries, one class at a time. Each is gated by the census (0 worse), FUZZ_SEEDS=150,
   and the callgrind gate.

Steps 4 and 5 are the bulk of the kernel work. They are weeks, not this session's milestones, and the
master should sequence them. Theorems 5 and 8 (the O1/O3/O5/O6 proofs) are **medium**. For quadrics
and tori they depend on `CurvedC`.
