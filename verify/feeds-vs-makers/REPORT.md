# Our feeds and speeds against the tool makers' published numbers

Generated by `cargo run -p feeds-vs-makers` (verify/feeds-vs-makers). Ratios are ours / theirs; each cell is the median (10th–90th percentile, count). Ours: forge_tooling::resolve with the maker's own preset OFF (generic group table, material class, tool substrate), at the maker's own ae/ap, no machine clamps. Theirs: CAM-HAAS's cited raw rows (research/cam/tooling/raw/*.json; each row's URL is in rows.tsv). Ranges are taken at their middle.

## Summary

- What it found and fixed: our generic carbide speeds were forgeCAD placeholders 1.5–2.5× over every maker's median (P 180 against 66–104 m/min, K 160 against 64, M 110 against 67–76, H 60 against 37), and holding Guhring's high-performance drill series for every carbide drill ran drills 2–3× faster than YG-1's and Lakeshore's tables. Both are now the makers' pooled medians, cited.
- Held out (each maker against a table derived from the other makers only, the independent check): guhring speed 0.41; harvey speed 1.27, chip 0.43; helical speed 0.77, chip 1.33; lakeshore speed 1.11, chip 0.93; yg1 speed 1.79. The makers disagree with each other by up to ~2.5×: Harvey publishes bigger chips than the others (0.43 means the others' chips are 0.43 of Harvey's), Helical smaller chips and faster speeds, YG-1 slower drill speeds, Guhring's carbide drills run ~2.4× the others' speed. Our pooled table sits between them; no maker is more than 3× from the others (CI band).
- Read the speed medians below with that in mind: they are calibrated to these rows, so ~1.00 is by construction. The independent evidence is the chip by op class, the chips of the groups not calibrated (M, K, S, H) and the physics checks; the CI test's job is to stop any later change from skewing a row silently.
- Over 3124 cited end-mill rows (Harvey, Helical, Lakeshore), our spindle speed is 1.00× the makers' at the median and our feed per tooth 1.00×.
- Per material group (median speed / chip, ours/theirs): P 1.06 / 0.76, M 1.11 / 0.93, K 0.81 / 1.32, N 1.04 / 1.20, S 1.00 / 0.73, H 1.41 / 1.11. A chip under 1 is conservative. H's rows are Helical's two hardness classes (ratio 0.77 on one, 1.41 on the other, every tool type alike): one H speed sits between them; a hardness-aware H speed needs the part's hardness (forge-material's next keys).
- By op class (chip): slot 1.14, rough 0.76, finish 1.47. Expected: makers allow a bigger chip roughing than slotting (we hold one chip per material), and their finishing chips are not raised for chip thinning while ours are (we hold the chip; they hold surface finish). Class-aware presets are next.
- Drills: 288 rows; all but 12 are rows our drill defaults were set from (a maker's own table or the pooled median), so they check drift, not independence.
- The 10th–90th spread is wide because the makers disagree with each other (e.g. wrought aluminium: Harvey's series at 1000 SFM, Helical's H45AL at 2100); a tool's own maker data (its preset) is used ahead of these generic numbers whenever it exists.
- Model checks against makers' own published adjustments: radial chip thinning matches Helical's and Harvey's formula (forge-tooling feeds tests); ball-nose effective diameter matches CNCCookbook's worked example; the slot chip and drill feed rise with diameter as the makers' tables do (rows by diameter, interpolated).

## Presets ON (the real default path)

Each row run again as an op of the language runs it: the tool's own maker preset taken by `Tool::preset_for` for the op's class (the representative "<group> <class>", i.e. the maker's row for the group's representative material; a maker's slot row runs as a slot, rough as a pocket, max as a clearing, finish as a contour, which `Tool::class_for` classes finish at a finishing width; the rest general), at the maker's ae/ap, no machine clamps. 3088 of 3412 rows have a preset for their group on their tool (the rest fall back to the generic table above).

- All: rpm 1.12 (0.63–2.60, n 3088), f 1.00 (0.57–1.67, n 3072).
- Reproduction: run with the preset written for that very row (its material text and engagement), 2532 of 2915 rows give a speed and feed inside the maker's printed range (ratio to the range's middle: rpm 1.00 (0.55–1.00, n 2915), f 1.00 (0.50–1.00, n 2903); the presets take a printed range at its low end, the maker's "start here", where this report takes the middle). This checks the inch conversion and that a preset's feed is read as the maker's programmed feed at its own width.
- What the spread below means: this pass names no stock material, so the representative preset stands for every material of its group on that tool (e.g. Harvey's wrought-aluminium row for a cast copper part), and rows of other materials differ by the maker's own material-to-material spread. When the part names its material (forge-material), `Tool::preset_for_material` takes the maker's row for it instead (alloy named in the maker's text, else its family pattern; the slowest hardness class among equals): tests `presets_by_stock_material_and_class` and `a_makers_tool_takes_its_row_for_the_stock_material`.
