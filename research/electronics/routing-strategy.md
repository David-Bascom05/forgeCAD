# Routing quality, priority and AI control

Research and design recommendation, 27 November 2026. Repository baseline `b16f21b`. ForgeCAD should answer a concrete question before an AI commits copper. **Which electrically acceptable alternatives exist for this connection, which other paths would each consume, and what evidence supports that answer?** The same evaluator can compare floorplans before detailed routing and score the copper afterward.

## What already exists in ForgeCAD

The schematic handoff emits occurrence-qualified terminals, footprint pad maps, populated components, differential pairs, and consumer/provider links with optional return endpoints and preferred distance. A PCB net class currently supplies minimum width and spacing. Board reports expose routed segments, through vias, physical connectivity, ratsnest and DRC witnesses. The MCP tests already exercise an atomic board edit and a DRC query against the updated geometry. Extend these native Rust paths. The routing requirements, negotiated search and agent operations described below are proposals. External routers, solvers and datasets belong in offline comparisons or verification. ## What competent routing preserves

The physical object under evaluation varies. A delay budget belongs to a path between pins, leakage can depend on a surrounding guard, and isolation belongs to an entire barrier. Several requirements can apply to the same piece of copper.

| Application | What to encode or measure | Evidence and limits |
|---|---|---|
| Source-synchronous memory | Define a strobe and its data members per lane, with endpoint pairs, allowed layers, topology and signed delay windows. Include applicable package delays. Reserve escape order and tuning space. | AMD UG863 specifies a DQ-to-DQS window of −50 to +50 ps for the documented Versal DDR5 interface and separate constraints per byte/nibble/strobe group. The clock-to-DQS windows also differ by component or DIMM topology. Bind the profile to its device and revision. [Timing](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug863-versal-pcb-design/Timing-Constraint-Rules-for-DDR5-Signals) · [Physical rules](https://docs.amd.com/r/en-US/ug863-versal-pcb-design/Physical-Design-Rules-for-DDR5-Signals) |
| Serial links and differential pairs | Route the two conductors together with legal spacing and a continuous reference. Track uncoupled breakout length, via transitions, unused via stubs and channel loss budget. Account for connector and package contributions where characterized. | Two matched routes can both have excessive insertion loss. Geometry gives witnesses for stubs and plane gaps. Channel-level loss and eye margin need appropriate models. [Intel high-speed serial design guide](https://www.intel.com/programmable/technical-pdfs/683864.pdf) · [KiCad 10 constraints](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html) |
| RF matching and antennas | Preserve a characterized launch or matching layout and its stackup assumptions. Give the RF feed a controlled cross-section. Apply the specific antenna's copper and enclosure keepouts where supplied. | Copying only the schematic values can change the matching network's parasitics. A reusable RF block needs pad orientation, local ground and geometry that can be locked together. [Nordic reference circuitry and layouts](https://docs.nordicsemi.com/r/bundle/ps_nrf52811/page/ref_circuitry.html) |
| Precision analog and instrumentation | Represent sensitive paths and aggressors separately. Measure parallel exposure using separation, layer and reference context. For an electrometer, represent the driven guard, its potential and coverage around the high-impedance input. | A fast-edged GPIO can be the aggressor even if its own timing is generous. A shunt sense route can have perfect connectivity and still include unwanted copper resistance. Thermal rise depends on copper, surrounding material and cooling. [ADI AN-139](https://www.analog.com/en/resources/app-notes/an-139.html) · [TI shunt layout](https://www.ti.com/content/dam/videos/external-videos/en-us/4/3816841626001/6076326896001.mp4/subassets/current-sense-amplifiers-shunt-resistor-layout-presentation-quiz.pdf) |
| Multidrop and slow control buses | A terminated RS-485 trunk has endpoint and stub constraints related to driver rise time. For I²C, account for pull-up, total loading and mode-specific rise time across the bus. Include off-board loading when known. | The same pins can form a legal electrical net in a star or a daisy chain. The topology matters to the first bus, and total capacitance can matter more than any individual branch length to the second. [TI RS-485 design guide](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slla272d/slla272d.pdf) · [I²C specification UM10204](https://assets.nexperia.com/documents/user-manual/UM10204.pdf) |

For an isolation barrier, check clearance through air and creepage along eligible insulating surfaces, including the applicable treatment of slots. | An ESD clamp on the correct net can still have a poor discharge path. Isolation limits require the applicable standard, working voltage, material and environment. Missing conditions leave coverage open. [TI ESD layout guide](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slva680a/slva680a.pdf) · [TI isolation guidance](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/sdaa268/sdaa268.pdf) |
| Rigid-flex and dense package escape | In a bend region, use the selected fabricator's layer, conductor-direction and via restrictions. Across a dense pin array, allocate escape channels, layer access and bus exit order as shared resources. | A short route can violate bend reliability. Individually escapable pads can still have an impossible combined exit order. These require regional and group constraints. ## Three meanings of priority

1. **Rule precedence determines which requirement applies.** KiCad custom rules have ordered matching and support minimum, optimum and maximum values. Altium xSignals allow a rule to target a source-to-destination path across a series component or along one branch. ForgeCAD should compile applicable rules into a resolved requirement with stable terminals, scope, metric, units, limit, source, assumptions and hardness. Intersect compatible mandatory limits and surface inconsistent ones with both sources. Preserve explicit override semantics on import. [KiCad PCB editor](https://docs.kicad.org/10.0/en/pcbnew/pcbnew.html) · [Altium xSignals](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/high-speed-design/xsignals)
2. **Routing order allocates the next search attempt.** Altium provides a 0–100 net routing priority. Freerouting documents classes routed early and protected from lower-priority rip-up. Both are useful controls. A difficult pad escape, a narrow timing margin or the loss of the last legal corridor should also raise urgency dynamically. Previously routed flexible copper may need to move. User-locked geometry remains fixed during the attempt. [Altium routing priority](https://www.altium.com/documentation/altium-designer/pcb/design-rule-types/routing) · [Freerouting settings](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting/blob/master/docs/settings.md)
3. **Acceptance determines which completed design is preferable.** Require continuity for every required terminal, fabrication legality and every evaluable mandatory electrical bound. Return unknowns with their missing inputs. Within feasible candidates, compare worst remaining margin in the project's highest criticality tier, then lower tiers, then ordinary costs such as length, vias and edit churn. Preserve alternatives when margins genuinely trade off. Give a human or agent the violated metric, path, location and magnitude.

**Worked decision.** Suppose an applicable lane profile permits ±50 ps. On one preliminary route the signed DQ-to-DQS difference is +43 ps including package delay. The nominal headroom is 7 ps. If the layer-delay estimate has ±10 ps uncertainty on that difference, the plausible range reaches +53 ps. A second candidate estimates +25 ±10 ps but adds 12 mm to an indicator connection. Assuming that GPIO meets its own rules and creates no new aggressor exposure, retain the second candidate ahead of the first. These are illustrative inputs. For a two-sided bound compute the smaller distance to either edge. Keep model uncertainty visible and normalize headroom only against a declared budget before comparing unlike units.

In a hand-drawn design, the active pin function and peripheral instance seed group membership. The project supplies topology, operating point and stackup. A library profile can supply datasheet relationships or reference geometry. A connector instance can carry application-specific intent. Route priority can also be *relational*: an indicator clocked at low frequency might tolerate a long detour but still need to yield clearance to an adjacent sensitive input because its edges are fast. An unannotated interface can receive ordinary geometric checks while its missing electrical profile is reported. [Existing design-intent contract](design-intent.md)

### Pin-function compatibility belongs in schematic ERC

For a direct signal, clock or interface contract, schematic ERC checks the active provider and receiver on each fitted branch. An inclusive set of nominal roles describes what the receiver needs from that driver. The default pin mode and occurrence-specific roles participate. Available inactive alternates can explain a fix. A passive tap contributes physical connectivity. It creates no functional receiver. The example contains a sensor, an MCU with GPIO and ADC modes, a fixed monitor input and a test point. [Executable example](../../examples/electronics/adc-fanout.fg)

### What a layout agent can infer

1. Query schematic ERC and the handoff for the same assembly variant. Use `signal_paths` to find a source terminal, receiver terminal, their active functions and declared contracts. The occurrence terminal joins directly to `components[].pads[].terminal`. A null function denotes the base library definition. A mismatched branch points to a schematic decision. An unknown branch has insufficient contract evidence. An entirely untyped net has no inferred signal path. Preserve the other verified branches.
2. A compatible branch identifies the pads that implement a function. Use the matched role ID to look up its design meaning. For example, a sensor and an ADC may share a library-defined measurement role, while a load on the same net has a different acquisition requirement. A matching role proves only the direct nominal contract. Path records can coexist with separate driver or voltage findings. The text of a pin name, role key or net label supplies no impedance, noise budget or timing limit.
3. A future routing profile can carry explicit electrical budgets keyed by stable role ID and scoped to a receiver or peripheral. Placement comparison already works from the existing schematic and board. Resolve it against board stackup, project constraints and operating conditions. Emit individual obligations with subject pads, metric, units, limit, hard or preferred status, and source. For an ADC path those might concern settling or separation from a named switching node. For a clock they might concern its return plane or permitted delay. A profile can require an operating point and report it missing.
4. Give the agent those obligations and their provenance when it proposes a placement, corridor or trace edit. Have the backend measure the result and report which margins improved or regressed per branch. Base scheduling priority on the tightest applicable margin and available routing space. Authored copper and pass/fail measurements remain reviewable after the proposal. The native floorplan comparator measures geometric placement estimates today. Electrical delay, settling and impedance budgets need their own models.

### Comparing candidate placements

`forgecad pcb BOARD floorplan [variant=ID] [NAME@GROUP=DX,DY ...]` compares translations in millimetres against the compiled source. A group is a board placement ID, an exact schematic occurrence or a sheet occurrence subtree. Append more `@GROUP=DX,DY` moves to the same candidate to move disjoint blocks together. Each candidate starts from the same board. The MCP `pcb` selector uses the same syntax. Querying a board never writes it.

The backend joins fitted terminal IDs to actual transformed footprint pads. It scores verified fulfillments and returns, active compatible signal branches, and both sides of an ERC-verified component path. When a verified signal path runs from an output through a component to a passive tap, input pins on the tap's net supply additional placement guides. This last inference identifies electrical topology. The report exposes its basis separately from active-function compatibility. A passive test point contributes to the net tree. A disconnected fulfillment, absent placement, pin mismatch or unknown contract is reported as missing evidence.

The version-one score combines three estimates. All weights are reproducible placement heuristics.

| Contribution | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Net extent | One Manhattan minimum spanning tree per net over physical pads. Each tree edge identifies both pads and sheet scopes. The weight is 1 for ordinary nets, at least 3 for explicit differential-pair members, and up to 8 for a declared minimum trace width relative to the process minimum. An unclassified net on a dedicated filled plane produces no generic attraction. |
| Endpoint locality | Nearest pad pair by Manhattan distance. A verified fulfillment or clock gets weight 4, a typed component path 3, and a compatible signal or input on a verified output network 2. A declared preferred length adds its positive excess to the cost. |
| Reference coverage | For same-side branch guides and explicit pair tree segments, project the straight guide onto the adjacent copper layer when it is a dedicated plane with one zone net. Clip against the actual filled triangles and union the intervals. The weighted uncovered length adds to the score. A null coverage measurement denotes unknown reference geometry. |

Each alternate recompiles the translated board, refills its zones and runs native DRC. Error findings other than pending routing block selection. Courtyards outside the perimeter and additional opens on an already tracked net also block selection. Equal scores retain input order with the baseline first. The report gives per-term deltas from the baseline, contributions between sheet scopes, missing evidence and absolute coordinates of the moved placements. Assembly variants filter electrical paths and pad-tree participants while preserving the common board artwork.

The [six-fan placement study](../../examples/electronics/fan-controller/six-amp-study.md) replays six candidates on a shared board. This command compares proposed group poses offline. Interactive incremental search, per-layer routing capacity, detailed escape and electrical signoff remain separate work.

### Searching inside placement blocks

By default `pcb repack` groups a compatible point-to-point clock and explicitly linked supply/return pairs using the fitted schematic. It first fits these parts against their internal pad guides, then places the complete local circuit. Its bounding rectangle reserves space around and between the members. Distributed clocks with multiple compatible sinks retain individual guides. The `independent` option disables automatic grouping. Repeated `circuit=REF,REF,...` options preserve known local templates. Overlapping declarations are joined. A fixed member locks the template. Instance poses in a named version can supply an alternate internal arrangement.

Native floorplanning reports the placement references crossed by each direct same-side local guide. Selection prefers fewer such crossings before the weighted score. Intersections are placement evidence. Actual routes can leave the layer and need their own clearance and electrical checks. In repacking, an incumbent containing outsiders in a reserved circuit is reported with `LocalCircuitInterleaved` and is ineligible.

The native `pcb repack` query accepts multiple named versions per physical block. A version provides seed coordinates for any number of footprints. Blocks can own disjoint portions of the same schematic sheet. The tool takes the Cartesian product of the versions, places the remaining footprints individually and returns reproducible poses, electrical costs, DRC blockers and combinations that failed to pack. The same query works through the PCB MCP tool. It reads the board without modifying the source.

For example, compare two port banks and two switch-pair orientations on the existing six-amp board.

```sh
forgecad pcb examples/electronics/fan-controller/six-amp.fg repack \
  fixed=J11,J12 gap=0.55 rotate \
  ports:north@J11=22,99,0@J12=49,99,0 \
  ports:opposite@J11=22,99,0@J12=49,7,180 \
  switches:inline \
  switches:crosswise@Q2=42,52,90@Q3=54,52,270 --json
```

The two coordinates are absolute millimetres. The angle is a rotation relative to the source in quarter turns expressed in degrees. `fixed` locks those footprints to the selected version. Other coordinates seed the search. `rotate` also searches orientations for freely placed footprints outside the named blocks. `moves_mm` and `angles_deg` give the final absolute values for edits. The baseline participates in the comparison. One invocation supports up to 256 combinations.

The packer uses the placed courtyards, holes and cutouts, indexes nearby packages in spatial bins and estimates electrical cost from specific pads. It starts with the baseline's net trees and typed branches. The final evaluator rebuilds these for each candidate. Packing currently handles a single populated top side before routing. Its rectangular search envelope approximates irregular geometry. Native outline and clearance checks determine finalist eligibility. Congestion, enclosure access and thermal headroom need separate evidence.

The [compact fan-controller study](../../examples/electronics/fan-controller/compact-study.md) also varies the board perimeter, mounts and pour together and adds explicit guides for its converter, local bypasses and fan ports. Those outline choices and device-specific guides live in the example. The packing solver and block-version interface are shared across boards.

## Native routing kernel

- **Global planning.** Evaluate several allowed block poses before committing long routes. Build a per-layer capacity graph from the outline, pad escapes, existing copper, via restrictions, filled references and obstacles. Give paired and ordered bundles their joint width and spacing envelope. Model the shared portion of a branched trunk once. Return estimated overflow, affected connections, scarce passages and alternative corridors. Coarse capacity estimates can miss pad-level bottlenecks.
- **Negotiated congestion.** Use A* or a maze search for candidate paths. Raise the cost of using oversubscribed edges and keep a history cost where congestion repeatedly reappears. Rip up a bounded set of editable routes and try alternatives. Apply a group-specific margin penalty on scarce paths. The global search may provisionally overbook a corridor. Exact copper remains subject to clearance checks before commit. PathFinder demonstrated the negotiation principle for FPGA resources. A PCB implementation must add continuous geometry, layer references and physical via footprints. [McMurchie and Ebeling](https://doi.org/10.1109/FPGA.1995.242049)
- **Specialized detail routing.** Use coupled pair primitives, ordered or flow-based package escape, topology-aware branches and explicit tuning regions. Refine guides into legal tracks, arcs and vias with exact local geometry checks, walk-around or bounded shove. Refill affected planes, reconstruct connectivity and re-evaluate electrical paths after detours or tuning. Keep curated RF structures and locked geometry intact. Unresolvable congestion should produce a placement or stackup alternative. Siemens sketch routing demonstrates a useful interaction in which a designer supplies a corridor and the tool builds the traces. [PCB escape research](https://doi.org/10.1109/TCAD.2005.857376) · [Siemens design automation](https://www.siemens.com/en-us/products/pcb/engineering-productivity-and-efficiency/design-automation/)
- **Interactive speed.** Index geometry by layer and tile, and maintain dependencies from a moved part or edited trace to its corridors, obligations and referenced plane regions. Update local legality and affected estimates on drag. Schedule negotiated reroutes and fresh fills in the background. Tag estimates, verified geometry and outstanding electrical checks with their board revision. Measure p95 and p99 edit latency and current coverage independently of whole-board solve time. ### Agent contract and first deliverable

Give the agent structured operations over a versioned board. **Inspect** returns resolved requirements, ownership and missing inputs. **Plan** takes a group, fixed geometry and optional routing guides and returns several feasible or blocked candidates. **Explain** identifies the contested passage, blocking objects, incumbent routes and margin before/after. **Apply** accepts a candidate ID against its base revision, writes a checked native edit atomically and returns the physical and electrical findings. Each candidate carries its seed, search budget, affected geometry, assumptions and estimator versions. The AI may try a rotation, a different escape, a permitted layer or a bounded rip-up. A proposed relaxation, pin swap or topology change goes through its own explicit design edit. Keep all checking and search available with no model service.

Start with a **read-only evaluator and a deterministic corridor router** for a bounded subset: ordinary nets, one paired path, a continuous reference requirement, locked geometry and a sensitive-path/aggressor spacing preference. Expose missing coverage for other obligations. Then compare three controls on identical inputs: sequential shortest-path routing, negotiated native routing, and an AI choosing among the native router's candidates. Later add a learned candidate ranker only if it improves the held-out results or reaches the same quality with less search.

Use paired fixtures that deliberately reverse the preferred choice: open a second legal passage, insert a reference-plane slot, change the active pin function, add a Kelvin tap on the load-carrying side, move a route into a dynamic bend or remove package-delay data. Track **full-board clean pass**, worst per-group margin, unsupported requirements, disrupted existing routes, candidate latency and compute per accepted board. Partition training and evaluation by design family, including repeated channels and board revisions. Report first-attempt and bounded multi-attempt results separately. Re-read final manufacturing artwork for opens, shorts and actual bottlenecks. For calibrated timing, RF, thermal, isolation or leakage claims, match each verdict to the appropriate model, review or bench evidence.
