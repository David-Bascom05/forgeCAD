## Joints (6.1)

```
door_f  = cylindrical(brk_b.pin, door.hinge, turn: door_f.turn, along: -9.92, turn_limit: (0, 105deg))   # R11
latch_f = cylindrical(hdl.latch_axis, door.latch, turn: latch_f.turn, along: 8.17 + latch_lift,
                      turn_limit: (0, 90deg), along_limit: (7.49, 8.17))
```
- Drives are joint params (`door_f.turn`, `latch_f.turn`); Forge has no free-standing drive names
  (`param door_f_turn` is a Syntax error), so the SPEC's `door_f_turn` is `door_f.turn` here.
- The lift is the shared param `latch_lift` (0, -0.68, -0.40); `latch_f.along = latch_rest + latch_lift`.
  It also picks the spring file for the state (8.17, 7.49, 7.77) with an `if`.
- The hinge is written bracket-first so a positive turn opens (SPEC issue 1, R11).

## Why generated

Forge subassemblies are rigid in v1 (forge-asm model.rs: "A subassembly document, rigid as a whole
in v1") and `subassembly()` takes no param overrides, so a parent cannot drive `door_asm.fg`'s hinge or
latch. `test.fg` therefore repeats the door's instances and joints, and `gen_asm.py` writes both from
one block. U is flattened into both, because `fit()` takes only top-level instances: the balls' seat
on the latch base's detent rims cannot be declared across the subassembly boundary (Forge gap 3).
`latch_u.fg` stays as U's own check.

## SPEC issues

**R12** (after R11, from Forge's new HardwareNearHole check): the latch base's S-M6-0 sat 7.5 from
the four detent holes (PEM 8.6). Replaced by a 6.6 hole and an ISO 10511 M6 nyloc on the back face
(anchor `pivot_back`); no other part changes. See SPEC R12.

All decided in SPEC R11 (cb5a41c) and applied: 1, 2, 3 (closed lift -0.40), 4 (studs 140 /
182.5 / 225deg), 5 and 6 (link radius 14.8), 9 (0.5deg), 10, 16 (4-hole bracket, post nuts at s 32.5);
the rest accepted with no design change. The list below is the record as raised.

Numbered for the coordinator. Nothing below was changed silently: every part follows the SPEC
numbers, and the violations they cause are listed in Results.

1. **Hinge turn sign (6.1, R9).** `door.hinge` (normal +k) and `bracket.pin` (normal +zb) both point
   +Z. With the door as A (`cylindrical(door.hinge, bracket.pin, turn: t)`), +t turns the door
   anticlockwise seen from above, which swings the latch edge INTO the rack. 6.1 says a positive turn
   opens. I wrote the joint bracket-first, `cylindrical(brk_b.pin, door.hinge, turn: t, along: -9.92)`,
   which opens on +t with limit (0, 105deg). Alternatives for the SPEC: keep door-first with
   turn_limit (-105deg, 0), or give both pin anchors normal -Z. R9's table (door-first, turn 0, along
   +9.92) needs one of these. The rear door is the same (turned about Z).
2. **Closed lift sign (6.1).** `along_limit: (-0.68, 0.40)` and "+0.40 closed" contradict 5.8's y
   table: closed puts U 0.40 deeper into the rack, the same direction as riding between detents
   (-0.68). With along measured out of the door (R4: 8.17 + lift), closed is lift -0.40 (handle inner
   face at y -7.77). I use lift in {0, -0.68, -0.40} and along_limit (7.49, 8.17).
3. **Ball centres (5.8 table).** "centres 6.40" and "6.40 + 0.68" do not match the cam faces in the
   same table. A 4.0 ball in a 3.0 detent sits 1.32 above the base's back face (y 5.86); riding on the
   face, 2.0 above it (y 6.54). Those give the table's cam faces (6.83 and 7.51 with the 0.97 offset).
   Change the row to 5.86 / 6.54.
4. **Door stud at rack angle 60deg vs the padlock tab slot (5.8).** The FH-M4-12 at r 28, 60deg (door
   h 569.98, k 438.25, hole 4.0) runs 0.75 into the tab slot (k 439.5..446.5): the two cuts merge in
   the DXF. The same position puts the base's 4.6 hole 0.83 from the tab's bend tangent line (shop
   needs 5.84): HoleNearBend on RACK-322. Suggestion, checked against the base's edges (0.91), the
   tab bend (5.84), the arc slot, the cam's sweep (rack angles 252..18deg at r >= 22) and the vents:
   studs at 140, 182.5 and 225deg (base holes 7.08 from the bend, 1.73 from the left edge).
5. **Handle link holes vs the fin bend (5.8).** The 4.5 holes at r 15, 135deg are 4.04 from the fin's
   bend tangent line (t2 shop: 4.15): HoleNearBend on RACK-321. The fin is modelled OUTSIDE the chord
   (v 18.5..20.38; inside it, Forge refuses: EdgeMoveCurve). Fix: chord at v 18.65, or links at r 14.8.
6. **Cam hub radius (5.8).** S-M4-0 at r 15 in a hub of R 21.9 leaves exactly PEM's 6.9 centre-to-edge;
   one of the two reports 6.9 < 6.9 (rounding): HardwareNearEdge on RACK-313. R 22.0 gives margin
   (the hub then reaches x 287.0, still 1.0 clear of the strike web at 288).
7. **Handle fin height (5.8).** "24.0 outer" is measured from the inner skins: 5.8's y table puts the
   fin tip 24.0 beyond the handle's OUTER face. Modelled so (fin tip y -33.65 closed). Its flat is
   47 x 66.5 (part list says ~61).
8. **Latch base to door (R4).** R4 says the base meets the door face to face, but with R1 the door's
   `lb_stud` anchors sit on the outer face (where the FH head is) and the base's `stud` on its front
   face: 1.52 apart. Joined with `slider(base.stud[0], door.lb_stud[0], along: 1.52)`.
9. **"Door at 10deg with the latch closed" (6.1, 8).** At 10deg the cam is about 100 in front of the
   rack, past the prong, and nothing overlaps (Forge: 0). Even 1deg moves the cam 9.9 out, past the
   5.71 prong. test.fg checks at 0.5deg (the cam 5.0 out, inside the prong): 43.8 mm3, so the
   closed latch stops the door. Suggest 0.5deg in section 8.
10. **Anchor names Forge refuses:** `axis` (handle, cam) and `ball[0..1]` (cam) are built-ins
    (ShadowsBuiltin). Named `latch_axis` and `ball_seat[0..1]`. The handle also has `link_in[0..1]`
    and `spring_seat`; the base has `stud_back[0..2]` (washer seats).
11. **SPEC 8 "clearance(latch_u, door) >= 0.5"** cannot hold as one number: the link posts run 0.20
    radial in the 6.4 slots. Checked part by part: handle and cam >= 0.5, posts >= 0.18 (0.19 measured,
    mesh chord 0.01).
12. **"base to vent slot 0.75" (5.8)** is the h gap to the slots that were left out (column 4, rows
    17..22). To the slots that remain, the base is 21.29 away and the tab slot 20.74
    (`clearance_vents.py`).
13. **Spring** is fastened to the handle (`spring_seat`), not the door: it sits between both, and the
    file per state makes its other end land on the door face. Same result as 6.1's wording.
14. **Side panel return cut-back:** SPEC 8 expects 2 false HoleNearBend (N4); the current Forge
    reports none. The flat's bend line is drawn 800 long, over the cut-back ends; the real bend is
    620 (Forge gap 6). The return's bend length on the order is 620.
15. **Strike seat and stop anchors** have no x in SPEC; +z used. `pin` on the bracket is kind bore,
    size 8, depth 1.88 (SPEC gives no depth).
16. **Bracket screw heads in the door's swing (5.7, 6.1). A real clash.** 6.1 checks the door's
    sweep (y 31.03 at about 50deg, at x -277.98, the hinge return's tip on a 17.03 radius about the
    axis) against the bracket leg (y 33.12), but the ISO 7380 M6 heads on that leg stand 3.3 proud,
    to y 29.82, at x -277.98: the return's tip runs 1.2 into all four heads (Forge: 0.71 mm3 each at
    50deg). The return tip sweeps y > 29.82 only within 6.3 of x -277.98, so heads (r 5.25) at
    least 11.6 off the axis in x clear it. Options: hng nuts at s 32.5 on the post with bracket holes at
    i = +12 and -12 (both, so one bracket serves top and bottom), or the pin axis moved 2 further
    out from the post. (A shorter hinge-side return does not work: the pin nut at n -14 needs the
    return to n -22.6 for its 8.6 edge distance.) test.fg keeps the failing expect so the clash stays visible.

## Forge gaps

Repros in `probes/` where a small one shows it.

1. **Built-in names shadow anchor names:** `axis`, `ball`, `disc`, `mid`, `hole`, `out` can't be
   bound, so SPEC anchor names `axis` and `ball[..]` are impossible.
2. **Subassemblies are rigid and take no params** (forge-asm v1): a parent can't drive a
   subassembly's joints. door_asm.fg's hinge and latch can't move in test.fg or asm/rack.fg; both
   need the door's instances inline (gen_asm.py does it here). Subsystem F will hit the same.
3. **`fit()` takes top-level instances only:** a contact with a part inside a subassembly can't be
   declared, and `fit(sub, part)` does not cover the subassembly's parts (the balls' detent touch
   stayed an Interference). Hence U is flattened.
4. **Shop checks skip holes in a sheet's base profile:** a hole drawn in the `sheet()` sketch 0.58
   from a bend raises nothing; the same hole as a `cutout` raises HoleNearBend
   (`probes/hole_near_bend_profile.fg`, `probes/hole_near_bend_profile_only.fg`). All holes here are
   cutouts for that reason.
5. **PEM checks ignore internal cuts:** a PEM's hole overlapping another cutout, or 3 from a hole,
   raises nothing (`probes/pem_overlaps_cutout.fg`); only outline edges and bends count. That is how
   SPEC issue 4 went unflagged on the door.
6. **DXF bend lines run the whole original edge** even where a cutout removed the flange and bend
   (side panel: 800 drawn, 620 real).
7. **An expect's limit can't be a measurement** (`x(a) = x(b)`: ExpectUnevaluable): the padlock
   coaxiality is checked against the rack point instead.
8. **Drive params belong to a joint's own freedoms** (`door_f.turn`); `param door_f_turn` is a
   Syntax error, and a lift can't be its own drive name: `latch_lift` is a shared param that sets
   `latch_f.along`. An `if` can't test a joint value (WrongType), only a param.
9. **(fixed in forge-lang since)** **`expect .. at` re-runs the whole document per line:** door_asm.fg takes about 460 s for 30
   lines, test.fg longer.
10. **`plane(at: ..)` needs a sketch point,** so a first sketch with normal -Z (the door's reference
    skin) is made by rotating a construction sketch on `offset(xy, -t1)` by 180deg.
11. **An `expect .. at` past a joint's limit is evaluated at the limit, silently.** A plain run at
    115deg is LimitViolated; the same value in an `at` measures the 105deg pose and reports no error
    (the first 115deg check here read 0 interference that way). Lift the limit in the `at` line.
12. **(fixed in forge-lang since: sheets are placed folded by default)** **A part file's unfold is a second body in an assembly** unless `part(.., body: "<sheet>")` picks
    the folded one. Without it the flat patterns floated through the rack and gave false
    interferences and wrong clearances. Every sheet part here is instanced by body.
